# Client-book SQL

SQL case study for a fictional European broker, **Northline**. The question a working student would actually be handed: **who pays for the platform, where is the money invested, and which onboarding cohorts went quiet?**

The clients, instruments, prices, and trades are simulated. The SQL is not. Every figure in this README is the output of a query in `sql/`, regenerated by `run_analysis.py`.

Python only builds the book and prints the grids. The queries use the standard library `sqlite3` driver. Nothing to pip-install.

## The book

| | |
|---|---|
| Clients | 450, one account each |
| Window | 2 Jan 2024 – 30 Jun 2026 |
| Currency | EUR |
| Instruments | 21 invented equities, ETFs, and bonds |
| Ledger | 9,693 trades, 8,224 cash movements |
| Invested AUM on 30 Jun 2026 | €18.3 million |

Segments are `retail`, `affluent`, and `hnw`. Profiles on the application are `conservative`, `balanced`, and `growth`. Countries are DE, AT, CH, NL, FR. A slice of retail clients is onboarded in July–September 2025 and then stops acting like the earlier cohorts. That is a behavior in the generator, not a column. The queries have to find it.

Fees, as coded in `generate_book.py`:

- Commission: retail €4.90 or 12 bps, affluent €8 or 8 bps, HNW €15 or 3.5 bps (the higher of the two).
- Custody, charged monthly on invested market value: 18 bps retail, 12 bps affluent, 7 bps HNW, annualised.

## What the SQL says

**The fee model flips as clients get larger.** Retail revenue is almost all commission. HNW revenue is mostly custody. Affluent sits in between.

| Segment | Clients | Commission | Custody | Commission share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Retail | 314 | €34,197 | €3,908 | 89.7% |
| Affluent | 99 | €16,789 | €8,521 | 66.3% |
| HNW | 37 | €9,457 | €10,162 | 51.8% |

**Turnover is not the same thing as activity.** In 2025, notional traded divided by average month-end invested AUM is close across segments. The order count is not. Retail did 4,024 trades to turn the book 0.91x. HNW did 377 trades to turn it 0.74x.

| Segment | Avg month-end invested AUM | 2025 notional | 2025 trades | Turnover |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Retail | €1.15m | €1.06m | 4,024 | 0.91x |
| HNW | €7.16m | €5.28m | 377 | 0.74x |
| Affluent | €3.48m | €1.99m | 933 | 0.57x |

**The stated risk profile shows up in the holdings, and only partly in the trade count.**

| Profile | Clients | Avg trades | Invested AUM | In technology equity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Growth | 179 | 23.6 | €6.30m | 59.8% |
| Balanced | 179 | 25.2 | €7.89m | 11.9% |
| Conservative | 92 | 10.5 | €4.07m | 0.0% |

Conservative clients trade less than half as often and own no single-name technology. Growth clients have 60% of their invested wealth in four tech names. Balanced clients trade just as often as growth clients, so "number of orders" is a weak suitability signal on its own. The holdings are the signal.

**Wealth is skewed, not a single-client firm.** On 30 Jun 2026 the largest 10 clients hold **25.3%** of invested AUM (an even split would give them 2.2%). The largest 20 hold **42%**. Client-level HHI is **0.0125** on a 0–1 scale. Diversified ETFs are the biggest sleeve at 32.5% of invested AUM; technology equity is second at 25.7%.

**The mid-2025 retail cohorts are quieter, and the comparison is censored on purpose.** Activity means a trade or a deposit in that calendar month. A horizon is reported only when the month has already happened, and only for cohorts with at least 12 clients.

Six-month activity retention:

- Cohorts opened in the first half of 2024: **49.4%** (small months, often 13–20 clients).
- July, August, September 2025: **33.3%** (n=42), **25.0%** (n=48), **30.6%** (n=36). Weighted, **29.4%**.

Twelve-month retention is not shown for those 2025 months. The sample ends in June 2026, and the query drops the unfinished horizon instead of calling it churn.

A 180-day "no trade" flag is the wrong churn definition for HNW clients: they are supposed to trade rarely, and the dormant query therefore tags several million euros of healthy HNW wealth. The retail 2025-H2 block is the one that looks like a failed cohort: 53 open accounts, about €253k still invested, no recent order. See `sql/09_dormant_clients.sql`.

Unrealized P&L by name is in `sql/08_unrealized_pnl.sql`. The cost is the lifetime average buy price. That is a screening tool. It is not a moving-average ledger once clients have sold and bought again. The query header says so.

## Schema

```text
customers 1───* accounts 1───* trades *───1 instruments 1───* prices
                      │
                      └───* cashflows
```

Cashflow sign is the client's: deposits positive, withdrawals and custody fees negative. Positions are not stored. Every exposure, turnover, and P&L query rebuilds quantity from the trade ledger.

`sql/00_schema.sql` creates the tables and indexes. The other files are one `SELECT` each.

| File | Question |
|---|---|
| `01_monthly_flows.sql` | Deposits, withdrawals, orders, commission, custody by month |
| `02_revenue_by_segment.sql` | Revenue by segment and country |
| `03_revenue_mix.sql` | Commission versus custody inside each segment |
| `04_cohort_retention.sql` | Activity retention at 3, 6, and 12 months, with right-censoring |
| `05_sector_exposure.sql` | Invested AUM by asset class and sector on the last date |
| `06_turnover_by_segment.sql` | 2025 notional / average month-end segment AUM |
| `07_concentration.sql` | Top-10 and top-20 share, and client HHI |
| `08_unrealized_pnl.sql` | Last price versus average buy price, by instrument |
| `09_dormant_clients.sql` | Open accounts with no trade in 180 days |
| `10_profile_versus_behavior.sql` | Stated profile versus trades and technology weight |

## Run it

```bash
python generate_book.py   # rewrites data/*.csv from seed 7
python run_analysis.py    # builds an in-memory SQLite database, writes results/
```

`data/*.csv` is checked in, so the queries can be opened without regenerating. `run_analysis.py` also writes `data/northline.db` (gitignored) for the SQLite CLI:

```bash
python run_analysis.py
sqlite3 data/northline.db < sql/04_cohort_retention.sql
```

## Assumptions a reviewer should argue with

- One account per client. The schema allows more; the generator does not use them.
- No shorts. Sells cannot exceed the position.
- Custody is a haircut on invested market value, not on cash. Cash is a constraint inside the generator and is not a table.
- Prices are a factor model with a fixed seed, not a market history. Do not quote them as real returns.
- Average-buy-price P&L mis-states basis after a sell followed by a later buy. Rankings, not client statements.
